## Candidate 1: Promoter share pledging as a risk/avoid overlay (not a buy signal)

**What it is:** Use SEBI-mandated promoter shareholding disclosures — specifically the pledged
fraction of promoter holding, and changes in that fraction over time — as a signal to avoid,
underweight, or exclude names with high/rising promoter pledge levels, rather than as a signal to
buy anything. This is a distinct data type and a distinct economic mechanism from the
promoter/insider *transaction* signal proposed in the 2026-08-21 run (candidate 2 there): that one
was about promoters buying/selling and inferring informed trading; this one is about promoters
using their shares as loan collateral, which creates a structural forced-selling/distress risk
that exists independent of any trade.

**Source(s):**
- Chauhan, Y. et al., "Stock Pledging and Firm-Risk: Evidence from India," IIM Bangalore working
  paper / symposium paper. https://www.iimb.ac.in/jaaf-symposium-2020/papers/stock-pledging.pdf
  (also on academia.edu: https://www.academia.edu/100522530/Stock_pledging_and_firm_risk_Evidence_from_India)
- "Promoter Share Pledging and Downside Risk: Evidence from Indian Listed Firms" (panel of Indian
  listed firms, 2009–2023 per search summary), ResearchGate:
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/408385174_Promoter_Share_Pledging_and_Downside_Risk_Evidence_from_Indian_Listed_Firms
- "The effects of promoter share pledging on stock price crash risk and financial performance:
  empirical evidence from India," ResearchGate:
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378335023_The_effects_of_promoter_share_pledging_on_stock_price_crash_risk_and_financial_performance_empirical_evidence_from_India
- "Does promoters' share-pledging deteriorate the quality of reported earnings? Evidence from the
  Indian market," *Journal of Accounting in Emerging Economies* (Emerald):
  https://www.emerald.com/jaee/article/doi/10.1108/JAEE-11-2024-0482/1358373
- "Impact of Change in Promoters' Shareholding Pattern on the Performance of Small-Cap-Value
  Equity Stocks in the National Stock Exchange of India," *International Journal of Financial
  Studies* (MDPI): https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/16/1/32
- "SHARE PLEDGING AND ITS CONSEQUENCES: A STUDY OF INDIAN FIRMS," Prime Database:
  http://www.primedatabasegroup.com/newsroom/SHARE_PLEDGING_&_ITs_CONSEQUENCES_A_STUDY_OF_INDIAN_FIRMS.pdf

**Claimed effect (source's claims, not verified by us):** Across these sources, the consistent
finding is: (a) higher promoter pledge intensity is associated with significantly higher future
stock-price crash risk; (b) pledging firms show materially worse downside-risk metrics (CVaR, VaR,
maximum drawdown) than unpledged peers; (c) pledging is associated with lower Tobin's Q, lower
dividend payouts, and (per the Emerald paper) *lower reported earnings quality*, consistent with
pledging promoters having an incentive to manage earnings upward to avoid margin calls; (d) one
paper finds a *negative* relationship between increases in promoter shareholding and forward
returns specifically in small-cap value names. We could not locate a clean, single-number
long-short portfolio return (e.g. "high-pledge minus zero-pledge decile spread = X%/yr") in any
source's abstract — the documented effect is consistently framed as *risk* (crash risk, downside
risk, volatility, earnings-quality deterioration), not as a raw return premium. That is an
important distinction for how this would actually be tested and used.

**Mechanism:** When promoters pledge shares as loan collateral, a share-price decline can trigger
a margin call; if the promoter cannot post additional collateral, the lender invokes the pledge
and force-sells the shares on-market, mechanically accelerating the price decline (a
self-reinforcing loop, distinct from ordinary informed-trading or fundamentals-based mechanisms).
This also creates an incentive problem: a promoter under pledge pressure has a motive to prop up
the reported numbers or delay bad news, which is the channel the Emerald earnings-quality paper is
picking up. Both are structurally plausible and specific to concentrated-ownership markets like
India — pledging as a corporate-finance practice is far more prevalent among Indian promoters than
among diffusely-held US firms, which is presumably why this literature exists almost entirely in
the India/emerging-market context rather than as an import from US findings.

**Our assessment of evidence strength:** Multiple independent India-specific academic studies
(IIMB working paper, an Emerald journal article, an MDPI journal article, at least two more via
ResearchGate) converge on the same direction of effect using different methodologies (event
study, panel regression with crash-risk/CVaR proxies, earnings-quality proxies) and different
sample windows, which is a meaningfully stronger evidentiary base than a single study — closer to
"replicated within one market" than "one paper." The mechanism is mechanical and well-understood
(forced-selling via margin calls is a documented phenomenon in India, not a hypothesized channel),
which is more concrete than most behavioral-bias mechanisms. The main weakness is that none of the
sources we could access gives a directly tradable return-spread number; what's documented is a
risk-avoidance case, which is still actionable (screen out / underweight high-pledge, rising-pledge
names) but is a different kind of signal than the entry-alpha signals this project's queue usually
evaluates, and would need to be framed and tested as such.

**Explicit confound check (per this project's own history):** The profitability factor
(REJECTED.md item 2) looked like alpha until it was shown to be a small-cap/high-beta risk premium
in disguise. Promoter pledging is very likely correlated with the same thing — financially
stretched promoters pledging shares are disproportionately going to be smaller, more leveraged,
lower-quality-governance firms, which is close to the small-cap/high-beta population already
implicated in that postmortem. Any test of this signal should explicitly control for size and beta
the way the profitability factor re-test did, before concluding the effect is pledging-specific
rather than a repackaged size/distress proxy. We flag this here rather than after the fact.

**Why it might not transfer / other risks:** (a) as above, the size/beta confound needs to be
ruled out directly, not assumed away; (b) pledge disclosure data quality and timeliness vary — the
signal is only as good as how promptly/consistently pledge changes are disclosed and captured;
(c) pledging is a lagging/persistent state variable (pledge levels don't change every quarter for
most firms), so the signal's turnover and the size of the effectively-tradable universe (firms
with any meaningful promoter pledge) need to be sized before assuming this is broad enough to
matter portfolio-wide.

**What data would be needed:** Promoter shareholding pattern disclosures (SEBI-mandated quarterly
filings, Regulation 31 of the SEBI LODR Regulations) showing total promoter holding and pledged
holding as a percentage, per company per quarter. This is public data available via NSE/BSE
corporate filings and shareholding-pattern disclosures, and via aggregators (e.g., Prime Database,
Trendlyne, screener.in surface pledge percentages already). This is a new data source relative to
the XBRL income-statement collector, but structurally similar in kind (a periodic regulatory
disclosure) and looks realistically obtainable.

**NOT YET TESTED — requires the project's permutation-test gauntlet before any deployment
consideration.**

## Investigated but not proposed this week

**Credit rating / credit-risk anomaly (Dichev bankruptcy-risk anomaly, Avramov-Chordia-Jostova-
Philipov "credit risk and cross-section of returns").** A well-documented finding (developed
markets) that low-credit-risk firms earn higher subsequent returns than high-credit-risk firms —
a "puzzle" relative to standard risk pricing. We looked at this because credit ratings (CRISIL/
ICRA/CARE) are a data type the project hasn't used. We are explicitly *not* proposing it: per the
search summaries, the flagship US paper attributes the effect almost entirely to the worst
performance of low-rated stocks that account for only ~4.2% of total market cap, concentrated
around rating-downgrade events, with the literature's own explanation pointing to "mispricing
generated by retail investors and sustained by illiquidity and short-sell constraints" — i.e.
this looks like a small-cap/illiquidity/distress proxy by the original authors' own account, which
is close to a structural repeat of exactly what killed this project's profitability factor. We
could not find an India-specific effect size to check whether the same concentration holds here.
Not worth proposing without a India-specific study that at minimum sorts on size/beta first;
recorded here so a future run doesn't re-propose it without addressing this directly.

**Accrual anomaly (Sloan 1996 and international-evidence follow-ups).** On the "untried data
type" list only loosely (it uses the same income-statement/balance-sheet building blocks as the
project's existing fundamentals work, just recombined into an accruals measure, so it's more
"new construction" than "new data type"). We flag mainly because of what we found *against* a
strong India result: an international-evidence replication study (Pincus, Rajgopal & Venkatachalam
2007 and related work) found the US zero-investment accrual portfolio earns ~8.4%/yr, but
explicitly notes India (along with Sweden) would need roughly a *quadrupling* of sample size to
reach statistical significance — i.e. the anomaly, if present at all in India, is weak enough that
the original cross-country study itself couldn't confirm it. Not proposing without a
dedicated India study showing a real effect; recorded so a future run doesn't retry this without
new evidence.

**Earnings-call / management-tone textual sentiment.** On the "untried data type" list (options-
implied, ownership-flow, and text/alt-data were named explicitly). International evidence (US-
centric) that conference-call linguistic tone predicts abnormal returns and volume is fairly
well established. We could not find a citable India-specific stock-level study with a concrete
effect size — search results returned general international literature and one unrelated Nifty-50
sentiment/NLP piece with no clear methodology or effect size reported. This is plausibly
obtainable (concall transcripts are publicly available for NSE/BSE-listed companies via
aggregators like AlphaStreet, and building an NLP pipeline is feasible), but we don't have
evidence yet that the effect exists in India specifically, as opposed to assuming it transfers
from the US. Worth a more targeted search in a future run (rather than proposing it on the
strength of the US evidence alone, which is exactly the kind of untested transfer-assumption this
scout is supposed to flag rather than pass through).

**Bulk/block deal disclosures (NSE/BSE).** A different ownership-flow data type from both prior
promoter-trading candidates. We are not proposing it because the India evidence we found has the
same structural flaw already flagged for the 2026-08-21 promoter-transaction candidate: the
documented abnormal returns (cumulative returns as high as ~7.5% around the event, per one 2004–
2012 study) are concentrated in the *pre-disclosure* window and are explicitly interpreted by the
source literature as evidence of front-running, not post-disclosure drift. A systematic strategy
can only act on the disclosed information, so the tradable post-disclosure effect (if any) is
exactly what isn't demonstrated here. Not a new angle beyond what's already recorded for promoter
transactions; not re-proposing.

**BRSR / ESG disclosures.** India's mandatory Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report
regime (SEBI, phased in for the top 1,000 listed companies from FY2022–23) is new enough to be a
genuinely untried data type. The one return-focused study we found (SSRN, India large-caps,
2020–2025) reports a weak, statistically insignificant correlation between ESG score and 5-year
CAGR (r ≈ 0.18, p ≈ 0.075) — i.e. the closest thing to direct evidence we found is a *null*
result, not a signal. Not proposing on the strength of a null; recorded so a future run doesn't
retry this without a reason to think the picture has changed (e.g., BRSR data maturing, more
disclosure cycles accumulating).

**FII/DII stock-level flow, and analyst estimate revisions (both re-checked this run per last
week's note to revisit with better search terms).** Same outcome as 2026-08-21: still no
India-specific, stock-level, out-of-sample study with a citable effect size for either. FII/DII
material remains dominated by aggregate-index-level commentary; analyst-revision material remains
US/developed-market-centric. Not re-proposing; a future run should try different search
terms/venues (e.g. IBES/Refinitiv India coverage specifically) rather than repeating this same
search a third time without a new angle.
